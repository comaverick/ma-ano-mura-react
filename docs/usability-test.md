# Grocery-planning usability check

Use this short guide with 3–5 people who actually plan household groceries. No participant sessions have been conducted yet; automated browser scenarios cover the mechanics but do not replace observing people.

1. Give the participant a meal budget and household size. Ask them to choose a meal and explain whether its estimate includes every required ingredient.
2. Ask them to mark ingredients they have at home. Check that they understand a pantry selection means they have enough for all chosen meals.
3. Choose a recipe with an unspecified frying-oil amount. Ask them to enter the quantity they actually use, change servings, then reopen the recipe. Check that the original recipe line and their saved measurement remain distinguishable.
4. Plan at least two meals that share an ingredient. Ask them to find the combined quantity and then the whole packs they would buy. Check that they can explain why the pack total differs from the ingredient subtotal.
5. Show a variable-weight product and an ingredient with no package quote. Ask whether the full checkout budget is confirmed, then ask them to copy or print the list on a phone.

Record the task, completion without help, time taken, points of confusion, expected versus displayed total, and suggested wording. Avoid recording names or other personal information. Prioritize failures to understand missing costs, pack rounding or pantry quantities ahead of visual preferences.
